The Sinner in the Mirror

Romans 3:9–20 · July 2026
Big IdeaThe law of God exposes our true, utterly sinful selves — all alike under sin’s power, not even one of us righteous — so that every mouth is stopped before the judgement of God.

Aim

  • See that ’no one is righteous, not even one’ includes us — so we drop both the curated highlight reel and the comparison game.
  • Let God’s law expose the venom in our speech — group chats, call-outs and all.
  • Respond with a healthy fear of God that drives us to Jesus, and into praying for and inviting our friends.

You’ll need: Bibles (CSB), pens, about 60–75 minutes.

Connect (10 min)

  • You open your phone camera and it’s accidentally on front-facing mode. Instant horror. Why are unfiltered images so confronting when we put so much effort into curating our feeds?
  • When someone gets called out online, the group chat lights up. Why is it so satisfying when someone else’s sin goes public — and so terrifying to imagine our own chats or search history read aloud?

Read — Romans 3:9–20 (CSB)

9 What then? Are we any better off? Not at all! For we have already charged that both Jews and Greeks are all under sin, 10 as it is written: There is no one righteous, not even one. 11 There is no one who understands; there is no one who seeks God. 12 All have turned away; all alike have become worthless. There is no one who does what is good, not even one.

13 Their throat is an open grave; they deceive with their tongues. Vipers’ venom is under their lips. 14 Their mouth is full of cursing and bitterness. 15 Their feet are swift to shed blood; 16 ruin and wretchedness are in their paths, 17 and the path of peace they have not known. 18 There is no fear of God before their eyes.

19 Now we know that whatever the law says, it speaks to those who are subject to the law, so that every mouth may be shut and the whole world may become subject to God’s judgment. 20 For no one will be justified in his sight by the works of the law, because the knowledge of sin comes through the law.

Context

Paul has spent Romans 1–2 proving that Gentiles without the law and Jews with the law both stand guilty before God. Verses 9–20 are his closing address: a chain of Old Testament quotations (mostly Psalms) that leaves nobody in the courtroom with anything to say. The “law” in vv.19–20 means the whole Old Testament — and its God-given job is not to save us but to show us our sin, like a mirror with no filters.

Discuss

  1. Count the ’no one / not even one’ statements in vv.10–12. What exactly does Paul say no one is or does?
  2. Vv.13–18 run head to toe — throat, tongue, lips, mouth, feet, eyes. What is the final diagnosis (v.18), and why does it explain every symptom before it?
  3. ’Under sin’ (v.9) means sin is a power that owns us (John 8:34), not just a few bad choices. How does that cut across ’I’m basically a good person’ or ’live your truth’?
  4. The centre of Paul’s quote-chain is ’no one seeks God.’ Why does God define righteousness by our relationship with him, not by comparing our stats with the person next to us?
  5. According to vv.19–20, what can the law do — and what can it never do? Why does that shut every mouth, including ours?
  6. Paul spends most of his time on speech: open-grave throats, deceiving tongues, vipers’ venom, cursing and bitterness. Where does verbal venom leak out for you — the group chat pile-on, the ’brutally honest’ comment, the roast at work or uni that goes one step too far?
  7. If no one seeks God unaided, then every Christian you know is a miracle — including you. How does that kill both superiority (’I’d never do that’) and despair (’I’m too far gone’)?
  8. Fight, flight, freeze or fawn — which is your default response to God? What would ’falling on your knees’ actually look like this week in a share house, an exam block, or a new job?

Live It Out

  • KOG 3·2·1·0 — Pray for 3 friends by name (uni, work, share house — no one seeks God unless he moves). Connect with 2 this week: a real conversation, not just reacting to their story. Invite 1 — Young Adults Camp is THIS Friday, 31 July–2 August: the easiest invite of the year. Leave 0 unloved.
  • Weekly challenge: swap one scroll session a day for Bible reading plus two minutes of silence — the mirror with no filter (James 1:22–25). Before sending anything spicy in a chat, re-read vv.13–14 first.
  • Memory verse: “For no one will be justified in his sight by the works of the law, because the knowledge of sin comes through the law.” — Romans 3:20 (CSB)

Pray

  • Be honest before God — silently name one way vv.13–18 describes you, and thank Jesus, who stood silent before his accusers so that our guilty mouths could be forgiven.
  • Pray by name for your 3 friends — that the God who alone makes seekers would grab hold of them, maybe even through a camp invite this week.
  • Pray for Young Adults Camp this weekend (31/7–2/8) — for every friend invited — and that God would raise up new serving hands among us for KOGKids, KOGMini and KOGYouth.

Leader’s Notes

  • Set the tone: joyfully point to Jesus. Guests and not-yet-Christians may well be along (especially with camp this weekend) — this passage indicts everyone equally, so never corner anyone. Model unvarnished honesty first; the leader owns the mirror before asking anyone else to look in it.
  • Q1–2: ’no one / not even one’ appears six times; vv.10–12 are a chiasm centred on ’no one seeks God’. The eyes come last (v.18) because no fear of God is the root cause — every other symptom flows from it.
  • Q5: the law is a straight-edge that shows how crooked we are; it gives knowledge of sin, never justification. Don’t rush to fix the discomfort — next Sunday’s ’But now’ (Romans 3:21–31) is coming, and it only shines against this dark backdrop.
  • Vision tie: ’from faith to faith’ (Rom 1:17) — we’re at 50 of 75 households serving, and every conversion among your uni mates and housemates is a miracle of grace, not a recruitment win.
  • Live opportunities for this group: Young Adults Camp 31/7–2/8 — follow up every invite tonight; the church is praying for 6 additional qualified assistants for KOGKids, KOGMini and KOGYouth this quarter — a concrete next step for anyone wanting to serve; new midweek fellowships are launching — groups like this one are how the church grows.
